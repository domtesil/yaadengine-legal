YaadEngine — Privacy Policy

Effective date: 10 October 2026
Publisher: Dom Tesilbirth D Shira ("I", "me")
Contact: domtesil@gmail.com

YaadEngine is a flashcard app for studying with spaced repetition. This page explains, in plain language, what the app does with your information, what it can and cannot access on your phone, and what I can and cannot see.

The short version

  • YaadEngine does not ask for or collect your name, email address or any other personal information.
  • There are no accounts, no sign-up and no login.
  • Everything you create in the app stays on your own device. I do not receive it, and I have no servers that store it.
  • The only thing the app can send out is optional, anonymous usage counts and crash reports, sent to Google. The app asks you the first time you open it. Nothing is sent unless you say yes, and you can switch it off at any time in Settings. These reports never contain your cards, decks, pictures or file names.
  • The app has no ads and no tracking. It does not read your advertising ID.
  • Your data is not copied to Google's cloud backup or to a new phone automatically. You move it yourself, with the app's own export and import files.
  • I never sell or share your data, because I do not have it.

What the app stores on your device

YaadEngine keeps the following only in the app's private storage on your phone, where other apps cannot read it:

  • the folders, decks and cards you create or import, and the card types you make or import (their fields, layouts and styling);
  • the pictures on your cards, including pictures you draw on or crop in the app's picture editor, and any pictures or fonts that came with an imported deck;
  • your study progress and review history, with the date and time of each review and the button you pressed. The app uses these to schedule your reviews and to work out your statistics, such as your streak, retention rate and upcoming reviews. All of this is worked out on your phone;
  • your settings, such as the theme, your reminder choices and times, and which folders are open.

None of this is sent to me or to anyone else.

What the app can and cannot access

The app asks Android for only these permissions:

  • Notifications: to show the study reminders you switch on.
  • Alarms at an exact time: so a reminder or a "card due" notice appears at the time you chose, and not a few minutes late.
  • Start after your phone restarts: so your reminders come back after a restart or an app update.
  • Vibration: used by notifications.
  • Internet and network state: used only to send the optional usage and crash reports described below. If you say no, the app sends nothing.
  • Keep the phone awake briefly, and Google Play install information: added by Google's reporting software, so a report can be sent reliably and Google can tell, for example, whether the app was installed from Google Play. They are used only for the optional reports.

The app does not ask for, and cannot use: your camera, microphone, contacts, location, phone number, advertising ID, or access to your files and photos in general. It also does not read other apps' data.

Notifications are plain reminders, such as "You have a review card ready for review". They never contain the text of your cards.

Usage and crash reports (optional)

The first time you open the app it asks whether it may send anonymous usage and crash reports. Until you say yes, nothing is sent. If you say no, nothing is ever sent. You can change your answer at any time with the switch Usage & crash reports in Settings. Turning it off stops all sending and makes the app discard its random ID.

These reports are made with Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics, services of Google. Google processes them for me and does not use them for its own purposes such as advertising. I use them to fix crashes and to see which features people use, so I can decide what to improve.

What is reported

  • Which of the four main screens (Home, Search, Statistics, Settings) is opened.
  • How many cards you answered in a review session.
  • That a file was imported (its type: CSV, Anki or YaadEngine, and the number of cards) or exported (a deck or a folder).
  • That a card or a card type was made (and whether the card type uses the app's own look or CSS).
  • Details Google collects automatically with these services: the app version, the Android version, your phone's make and model, your language, the approximate country or region (worked out from your internet address, which Google does not keep), when the app was first opened, and how long it is used.
  • When the app crashes: the kind of error and where in the program it happened, your phone's make and model, the Android version, and the phone's state at that moment (for example free memory).
  • A random ID that the app makes for itself. It is not your name, your email, your advertising ID or your phone's number, and I cannot tell who you are from it.

What is never reported: the text of your cards, the names of your decks or folders, your pictures, the names of files, your study history or progress, your contacts, your location, or anything you type.

How long: usage reports are kept for 2 months and crash reports for up to 90 days, after which Google deletes them automatically. Only I can see them. More about how Google handles this data: [Google's Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy) and [Firebase and privacy](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy).

If you are under 16, or younger than the age at which you may agree to this where you live, please ask a parent or guardian before you say yes.

Pictures

When you add a picture, the app opens your phone's own photo picker or camera app. The app receives only the picture you choose or take. It copies that picture into its private storage so your card can show it. If you edit the picture in the app, the edited copy is stored the same way. Deleting the card removes its pictures from the app.

Files you import, open, share or export

You choose when any file goes in or out:

  • Importing: you can import cards from CSV files, Anki decks (.apkg), or YaadEngine files (.yaad). You pick the file in Android's own file chooser, or you send it to YaadEngine from another app using Share or "Open with". The app receives only that one file.
  • Temporary copies: to read a file, the app makes a temporary copy in its private cache. An Anki file is also unpacked there while it is read. The app clears older copies the next time you import, and your phone can clear them at any time.
  • Exporting: you can export decks and folders to a YaadEngine file, with or without your study progress. You choose where it goes: you can save it to a place on your phone or in your cloud storage, or share it through Android's share sheet to another app or person. That file, and whatever app or service you send it to, is then outside my reach and is handled by that app or service, under its own policies. I do not receive it.
  • Exported files are not encrypted. Anyone who gets the file can read the cards in it. Please share them only with people you trust.

Imported decks from other people

A deck that comes from someone else, such as an Anki deck, can include pictures, fonts and card styling made with HTML and CSS. YaadEngine shows such cards in a locked-down page that is inside the app. That page cannot open websites or load anything from the internet — a rule inside the app, which applies even though the app can send the optional reports — and links inside a card do nothing. As a result, a deck cannot track you or send anything out when you study it.

Backups

I have turned off Android's automatic backup and device-to-device transfer for this app. Nothing from YaadEngine is copied to Google's cloud backup or moved to a new phone automatically. To keep your cards safe, or to move them to a new phone, export them to a YaadEngine file and import that file on the other phone.

Links to other services

The Settings screen has two links that open in your web browser, and the Privacy Policy link opens this page:

  • Help & Feedback opens a form hosted by Jotform.
  • Suggest a Feature opens a feedback board hosted by Featurebase.
  • This Privacy Policy is a web page hosted by GitHub Pages.

The app sends nothing to these services. If you open a link, your browser contacts them, and what you type there is handled by them under their own privacy policies, not by the app. They may also record normal web-visit details, such as your internet address. Please do not include sensitive personal information when you write to me.

Open-source software

YaadEngine is built with open-source software, and the maths formulas on cards are drawn by a library that is packed inside the app, so they work offline. The Settings screen lists these projects and their licences. Apart from Google's reporting software described above, which sends reports only if you agree, none of them receives your data.

How your data is protected

Your data is kept in the app's private storage, which Android protects from other apps. The app does not add its own encryption or password. Anyone who can unlock your phone and open YaadEngine can see your cards, so please use your phone's screen lock. Files you export are not protected, as explained above.

How long your data is kept, and how to delete it

I keep none of your cards or study data, so I have nothing of that to delete on my side. The optional usage and crash reports are deleted by Google automatically after the time given above, and switching the reports off in Settings stops new ones. They are not tied to your name or email, so I cannot look up "your" reports, but if you have a question or want help, write to me. On your phone, your data stays until you remove it:

  • delete any card, deck or folder in the app, and its pictures go with it;
  • remove everything by uninstalling the app, or by choosing "Clear storage" for YaadEngine in your phone's settings;
  • files you exported or saved yourself stay where you put them until you delete them.

Children

YaadEngine does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. It has no accounts, no chat and no purchases. The only thing it can send is the optional, anonymous usage and crash reports above, and only if the user agrees; children should ask a parent or guardian first.

Your rights

Laws such as the GDPR in Europe and the UK, the CCPA in California and India's data protection law give people the right to see, correct, delete or move the personal information a company holds about them. Because I hold none of your cards or study data, and the optional reports are anonymous, there is little for me to show, correct or delete. You already have full control of your data on your own phone, and where the law asks for your consent (as in the EU and the UK), the reports are sent only with your consent, which you can withdraw at any time in Settings.

Changes to this policy

If the app ever changes in a way that affects your privacy, for example if it starts to send other information, I will update this page and the effective date above, before the change reaches you.
